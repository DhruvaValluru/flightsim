cause (measured by the builder on the two telemetry files): the two
recorders do not share a clock origin, and `compare()` interpolates on each
host's absolute `t`. `headless.json` (written by `gate5_ue_parity.py`
`write_reference`, the Python core's recorder) starts at t = 4.875 s and
ends at 64.875 s: the raw JSBSim clock, which has already run through the
trim before the hands-off flight begins, at a period of 0.1077 s (the
harness's own comment at `gate5_ue_parity.py:293` notes the period). The
UE telemetry starts at t = 0.008 s: the commandlet's clock is zero at the
latch. So the harness compares headless flight time x - 4.875 with UE
flight time x, and on a hands-off spiral that 4.9 s shift is the whole
residual (40.7 m, 46.5 deg of heading). Re-running the harness's own
`_unwrap_degrees` / `_interpolate` / `_wrapped_difference` with the headless
origin shifted to its first sample (elapsed = t - t[0]) gives, per channel:
altitude 0.0015 m, TAS 0.0002 kt, roll 0.0039 deg, pitch 0.0001 deg, heading
0.0010 deg, n_z 0.0000, lat 0.0000, lon 0.0000: every channel inside
`TOLERANCE` by two to four orders of magnitude. The integration therefore
agrees; the comparison's time base does not. No tolerance was loosened and
the headless reference was not edited.

Decision 14 (`decisions.md`): parity is graded on elapsed hands-off time,
each host's `t` taken from its own first sample, so the harness aligns the
origins (or the headless recorder writes elapsed time). Mac scope
(`experiments/gate5_ue_parity.py` and/or the core recorder); the overlap
line then reads the full 59.9 s.
